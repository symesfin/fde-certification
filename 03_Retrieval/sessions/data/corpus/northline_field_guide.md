# Northline Broadband — Company Field Guide

Northline Broadband is a regional broadband and mobile operator serving households and small businesses across three regions — North, Central and Coast. This guide is the plain-facts reference the service desk uses when a question is about the company itself rather than a fault, a tariff or a procedure. It is maintained by Knowledge Operations and reviewed every quarter.

## Company Overview and Mission

Northline Broadband provides full-fibre and fixed-wireless broadband, home phone, and a mobile service resold on a national carrier's network. The stated mission is "a connection you do not have to think about": the company competes on reliability and support rather than on price, and most of its marketing claims rest on the published regional latency targets and the first-time-resolution rate of the service desk.

## Founding and Leadership

Northline was founded in 2011 as a fixed-wireless operator covering the Coast region, and moved into full-fibre in 2016 after acquiring a municipal fibre build in the North region. The company is led by a Chief Executive, a Chief Technology Officer who owns the network and the NOC, and a Chief Customer Officer who owns the service desk, field engineering and retention. Regional operations are run by three Regional Operations Managers, one per region.

## Headquarters and Offices

The head office and the primary Network Operations Centre (NOC) are in the Central region. Field engineering depots are located in each of the three regions, and the service desk is split between the head office and a second site in the North region so that a single-site outage cannot take the desk offline.

## Headcount

The company employs around 410 people. The support organisation is 105 people: 45 field engineers, 30 first-line agents, 18 second-line engineers and a 12-person NOC. Second-line engineers own fault diagnosis once first-line checks are exhausted, and are the main users of the runbooks and the diagnostics assistant.

## Customers and Regions

Northline serves roughly 180,000 connections. The North region is the largest by connections and the best performing on latency; the Coast region is the smallest, is served partly by fixed wireless, and is the source of most reliability tickets. The Central region hosts the head office, the NOC and the core network.

## Why Customers Leave

Exit surveys from the save desk give three reasons for most 2024 cancellations, in this order. A billing surprise — a price-list change, a double charge, or a failed direct debit that became a restriction — is the largest, and it is the reason the save desk now sees every billing complaint before it becomes a cancellation. A fault that stayed open past five working days is second, and is concentrated in the Coast region. Moving to an address Northline cannot serve is third and is not a retention problem at all; it is a coverage problem, and it is why the Coast fibre migration is tracked by the retention programme as well as by the NOC.

## Services Overview

Three product families: residential full-fibre broadband (the Fibre Home tariffs), business full-fibre (the Fibre Business tariffs), and the legacy fixed-wireless service in the Coast region, which is being migrated to fibre street by street. Mobile is sold as an add-on to any broadband tariff.

## Company Values and Culture

The service desk works to three written values: say what you know and what you do not; never guess a code; close the ticket with the customer, not on the customer. The third value is why first-time resolution (FTR) is the headline desk metric rather than handling time.

## History and Milestones Timeline
